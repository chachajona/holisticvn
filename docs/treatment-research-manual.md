## Châm khô (`dry-needling`)

The current page explains the needle and proposed mechanisms but lacks practical safety information. Its short description promises recovery of neuromuscular function without defining a condition or outcome. The earlier proposed FAQ attributes serious complications mainly to insufficient training; that causal claim should be removed.

The replacement explains what is inserted, why there is no injected medicine, and that the method usually sits within broader rehabilitation. FAQs address sensations, information to disclose and serious chest symptoms. We deliberately do not give an incidence estimate for pneumothorax: the sources do not provide a dependable clinic-specific rate. Evidence of adverse events being underreported also rules out treating an absence of reported events as proof of safety. [Cleveland Clinic patient education](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/16542-dry-needling) supports the basic description and practical disclosure questions; [the 2026 umbrella review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12906-026-05607-9) supports cautious effectiveness wording.

The [2024 original Ghent case series](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/01HZ0DH92HM7WJG673XYFGP72D/01J2EHYXE6JRVEE4EDPNTG07AQ) documents pneumothorax after neck/shoulder needling, with chest pain and shortness of breath. A case series establishes that the complication occurred; it cannot estimate its frequency without a denominator. The FAQ therefore names the complication and advises urgent assessment for those symptoms.

The screenshot's treatment-bed photograph is a real clinic image, but it does not show dry needling. Keep its truthful clinic caption and seek a suitable treatment photo. Keep duration and step-by-step clinic procedure absent. Verify the clinician's actual qualifications separately from the existing `requiresCertifiedPractitioner` flag.

## Giác hơi (`cupping`)

This page needs the largest cleanup. The current description replaces detox language with another unverified explanation involving oxygen shortage, fascia stretch and tissue-pressure release. Legacy Sanity fields still contain Qi/blockage and broad healing claims. Public UI currently also displays an unverified 45-minute duration, seven-step procedure and an “an toàn và ít tác dụng phụ” benefit. Remove unverified clinic fields and prevent legacy full-description fallback from resurfacing them. Translate the English SEO without dropping its existing image asset.

The proposed copy keeps the observable suction mechanism and distinguishes dry from wet cupping as general education. It does not imply Holistic offers wet cupping. Benefit wording concerns possible pain relief, with limits for function and follow-up. The [NCCIH overview](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/cupping) supports a cautious benefit statement and concrete skin risks; [the 2025 systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12121573/) concerns immediate pain outcomes and does not establish functional recovery.

Source caution: [Cleveland Clinic's cupping page](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/16554-cupping) contains speculative detox assertions as well as useful practical material. Only its sections on capillary marks and seeking medical care are used here. Its positive detox, cardiovascular and other broad mechanism assertions are not adopted. The statement that marks cannot measure “toxins” is an evidence-based editorial inference: the sources describe a suction-related skin response and provide no validated toxin measurement. It is not presented as a direct quotation from NCCIH.

The photograph shows post-cupping marks and matches that subject; it is not evidence of successful treatment. Useful remaining questions are the exact cupping technique used, the provenance of the duration/procedure, and the clinic's own aftercare instructions.

## IASTM (`iastm`)

Remove the claim that instruments necessarily work deeper or more precisely than hands, as well as unconditional fascia elasticity/release and circulation claims. Possible changes in pain or motion do not prove a specific tissue mechanism. The revised introduction defines the technique and explains that many studies used other treatments alongside it. [The 2025 pain/function meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12891-025-08492-4) and [2024 range-of-motion review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12891-024-07452-8) support limited, qualified benefit wording.

The new questions address meaningful patient choices: whether a tool is always better, what health information matters, and what to do with excessive pain. The [2025 Delphi study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11941819/) provides original expert-consensus safety considerations. It is not controlled evidence quantifying harms; the copy asks for disclosure/assessment instead of presenting its list as a universal clinic exclusion policy.

A [2026 meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40001-025-03752-4), published 2026-01-19, was opened as a currency check. It also reports heterogeneity, and its GRADE narrative has inconsistent downgrade counts. No numerical estimate or certainty label from that paper is used in patient copy. The proposed cautious wording remains consistent with the overall picture.

The real Holistic instrument-on-skin photo matches the method. It does not identify a brand, credential or outcome. Confirm lubricant/tool allergy considerations and local aftercare before adding clinic-specific instructions.

## Tapping (`tapping`)

The spelling is ambiguous: the present description and illustration refer to adhesive **taping**, not a tapping exercise. Keep the supplied title and slug, explain the elastic-band meaning in prose, and ask the clinic before renaming. Remove generic promises of muscle-tone adjustment, swelling reduction and joint stabilization. A patient leaflet describing a particular indication is not evidence that every use has those effects.

The revised introduction separates the tape's physical properties and sensory feedback from uncertain clinical benefit. The shoulder example is explicitly condition-specific, based on [Cochrane's rotator-cuff review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD012720_kinesio-taping-rotator-cuff-disease). Skin precautions, removal for irritation and clinician-taught application follow [Royal Berkshire's March 2026 hand-therapy leaflet](https://www.royalberkshire.nhs.uk/media/ao0ealui/kinesiology-tape-for-hand-therapy_mar26.pdf). No fixed wearing duration is transferred from NHS care pathways to Holistic.

The temporary stock photograph shows elastic tape on an upper back and matches the method. Its visible “Ảnh minh họa” label must remain. Replace it with a clinic-owned photo when one is available. Confirm whether the service includes rigid tape, which product is used and what local skin/wearing instructions are given.
