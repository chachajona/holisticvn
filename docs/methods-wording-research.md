# Method-group wording research

Researched 2026-10-12. Scope: explanatory descriptions for five groups on a Vietnamese clinic site. This is a copy evidence note, not a treatment recommendation or an efficacy review. Sources are seven first-party health-service/professional-body patient resources. They explain techniques; they do not establish Holistic's outcomes or staff qualifications.

## Findings and optional English wording

### 1. Manual therapy and complementary techniques

Manual therapy means hands-on massage and work on an affected area's movement. NHS guidance supports explaining this as part of physiotherapy, alongside exercise and advice. Do not promise correction of spinal/pelvic alignment or removal of nerve compression. [NHS: Physiotherapy](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/physiotherapy/)

Dry needling inserts thin needles without medication into muscle areas; it may be used for pain and movement problems within an overall plan. It is distinct from a purely hands-on technique. [APTA ChoosePT: Dry needling](https://www.choosept.com/health-centers/dry-needling)

Cupping creates suction on the skin. NCCIH says evidence for pain relief is weak and other benefits cannot be established from high-quality research. Describe the technique; omit detoxification. [NIH NCCIH: Cupping](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/cupping)

**Optional wording:** “Hands-on massage and movement techniques for areas that feel painful or stiff. Depending on your assessment, the plan may also include specialised tools or complementary techniques such as dry needling and cupping.” The adjunct inventory comes from the brand; the description does not claim equal effectiveness for every technique. Keep “Tapping” out of explanatory examples until confirmed.

### 2. Treatment with devices

TENS sends a weak electrical current to nerves for pain relief; therapeutic ultrasound uses sound waves. These should not be described as the same mechanism. [NHS: Physiotherapy](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/physiotherapy/)

NMES uses stimulation to produce muscle contractions. Leeds' patient guidance concerns pelvic-floor weakness specifically: it supports this mechanism, not a general claim that stimulation restores all muscle control or proof that Holistic offers NMES. [Leeds Teaching Hospitals: NMES](https://www.leedsth.nhs.uk/patients/resources/neuromuscular-electrical-stimulation-nmes/)

Shockwave uses acoustic waves and is offered for selected persistent tendon/plantar-fascia problems, often after conventional care has not helped. Kent includes exercise in management and notes immediate relief can be short-lived. Avoid “rapid repair” or a general healing promise. [Kent Community Health: Shockwave](https://www.kentcht.nhs.uk/leaflet/extracorporealshockwavetherapy/)

**Optional wording:** “Device-assisted treatment selected for your condition: electrical pulses may help manage pain or activate muscles, while therapeutic ultrasound and shockwave use sound waves. The appropriate method depends on your assessment.” Prefer a device umbrella to an all-electrical label. Confirm modes before naming TENS/NMES publicly. Electroacupuncture is another listed service; no separate efficacy claim was established here.

### 3. Rehabilitation exercise and strength training

NHS describes exercises for joint movement and muscle strength, with regular practice contributing to rehabilitation. [NHS: Physiotherapy](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/physiotherapy/)

The brand distinguishes personalised rehabilitation for injury/pain/limited mobility from training to build strength and movement performance. Explain that difference by purpose, without suggesting rehabilitation excludes strengthening or strength work is only for professional athletes. The distinction below is an editorial synthesis of the brand offer and NHS guidance, not a clinical boundary.

**Optional wording:** “Exercises matched to your current ability and goals. Rehabilitation focuses on rebuilding movements affected by pain or injury; strength training develops strength and control for daily activities and sport.” Avoid guaranteed prevention of recurrence, permanent posture correction, or fixed recovery times.

### 4. Cold plunge

Cold-water immersion may lessen muscle soreness after a demanding workout. Cleveland Clinic frames it as an optional method, notes cardiovascular/cold-exposure risks, and cautions that use after strength training can interfere with muscle gains. Its advice does not support a blanket promise of faster recovery or improved nervous-system endurance. [Cleveland Clinic: Cold plunges](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/what-to-know-about-cold-plunges)

**Optional wording:** “Immersion in cold water that may help ease muscle soreness after exercise. Suitability and timing depend on your health and training goals.” Do not provide a universal temperature/duration prescription or extend exercise-soreness claims to injury healing.

### 5. Infrared lamp

Taiwan's Ministry of Health and Welfare/TFDA describes infrared lamps as devices delivering **local heat**, with burn/eye precautions and a requirement to follow device instructions. Its discussion of marketed pain-relief claims is regulatory guidance, not trial evidence of those outcomes. [MOHW/TFDA: Infrared lamp guidance](https://www.mohw.gov.tw/cp-16-67526-1.html)

**Optional wording:** “A lamp directs infrared energy onto the treatment area to provide local warmth.” If adding relaxation, identify it as the brand's intended comfort purpose, rather than a verified clinical result. No lamp-specific outcome evidence sufficient for a stronger claim was established in this bounded research. Do not borrow ultrasound, laser, hot-pack, sauna, or general rehabilitation evidence to assert this lamp repairs tissue, accelerates recovery, or stimulates the body's self-healing.

## Copy decisions still needing brand confirmation

- What “Tapping” means and which Vietnamese term accurately describes it.
- The actual electrical device/modes and whether naming TENS/NMES would be accurate.
- The infrared device/model; a heat-lamp description should not be repurposed for a laser or sauna.

For first-time visitors, lead with the action they can picture, then the limited purpose. In Vietnamese, prefer concrete explanations such as “bài tập theo tình trạng vận động,” “xung điện,” “sóng âm,” “ngâm nước lạnh,” and “làm ấm vùng được chiếu”; preserve confirmed brand service names in supporting labels. Use “có thể” for variable symptom relief and “nhằm” for a treatment aim. Neither word makes an unsupported mechanism or guaranteed outcome acceptable. No code, CMS, pricing, or task-status change is implied by these notes.
